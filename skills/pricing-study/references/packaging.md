# Packaging

- **Good-Better-Best.** The entry tier wins volume, the middle tier is the default you want most people on, and the top tier anchors value and captures high willingness to pay. Often 3 tiers, plus Free and Enterprise in B2B.
- **Fences** must follow real segment differences (usage, seats, sophistication), not arbitrary feature withholding.
- **Free tier tests:** what share of the base is on Free? What is the Free → paid upgrade rate? What is the paid → Free downgrade rate? If downgrades go straight to Free, the Free tier is too generous, or the entry paid tier is priced too far above it.
- **Value metric:** charge on the unit that grows with the value the customer gets (per subject, per location, per API call). Use a cap plus overage where cost-to-serve varies widely.
- **Bundles:** if the willingness-to-pay correlation between items is high (customers who want one also want the others), bundle them. If they are distinct segments, sell them separately.
- **Migration** of existing customers: plot regional phasing × soft vs forced migration as a 2×2. Rate the risk in each cell. Set a go/no-go checkpoint about 3 months in.
